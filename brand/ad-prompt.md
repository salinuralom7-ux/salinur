## Full brief

---

You are making advertising for **Nearse** (nearse.in). Read this before you
make anything.

**WHAT NEARSE IS**
Nearse is an online marketplace that connects people in Guwahati, Assam, India
with skilled local workers who already live near them — maids, cooks,
electricians, plumbers, carpenters, masons, AC and appliance technicians, home
tutors, drivers, beauticians, tailors, photographers, packers and movers,
gardeners, pet groomers. 155 services across 16 categories, covering 124
localities of Guwahati.

**HOW IT WORKS**
A customer searches for what they need. Workers nearest to them appear first,
each with their own rate already shown. The customer picks the service, the day
and the time, and the request goes straight to that worker on WhatsApp. The two
of them agree the final amount directly. Nearse is never in the middle of the
money.

A worker creates a profile in about two minutes: up to three services, their
own price for each, a photo, and a WhatsApp number they confirm. A person at
Nearse reviews every profile before it goes live.

**WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT — these are the selling points**
1. **Zero commission.** Nearse takes nothing from a worker's earnings and
   charges customers nothing to book. The rate shown is the rate the worker set.
2. **The worker sets the price.** Not the platform. This is the heart of the
   brand.
3. **A real person checks every profile** — the photo, the details, the number.
   Not an algorithm.
4. **The booking goes straight to WhatsApp.** No app to learn, no chat system
   to sign up for. Every worker in Guwahati already uses WhatsApp.
5. **Three months free for workers**, with no card, no UPI mandate, no payment
   details of any kind asked for. Nothing that can auto-charge, nothing to
   cancel.
6. **Hyper-local.** Guwahati only, by choice. Locality by locality — Jalukbari,
   Six Mile, Beltola, Ganeshguri, Zoo Road, Paltan Bazar, Maligaon, Chandmari,
   Hatigaon, Dispur, Uzan Bazar, Khanapara.

**WHO IT IS FOR**
*Customers:* households in Guwahati — working couples, families, elderly
parents living alone, students in rented flats, small shop and office owners.
Ages roughly 25 to 60. They speak Assamese and Hindi, read English. Their
current method is asking a neighbour and hoping.

*Workers:* skilled trades and service people in Guwahati, ages roughly 20 to
55, men and women. Many are very good at what they do and have no way to be
found beyond word of mouth. They are not poor or helpless — they are
professionals without a shopfront. Show them with dignity and competence.

**BRAND**
Name: **Nearse**. Said "NEAR-se". It comes from *near*.
Tagline: **Trusted people for every job, right where you are.**
Alternate line: **Every skill deserves an earning.**

Logo: a gold location pin, and inside it a worker wearing a hard hat and
dungarees. Gold on near-black.

Colours — use these exactly:
* Background / near-black warm charcoal: **#0C0A07**
* Brand gold: **#EABB51**
* Light gold: **#F3D07C**
* Warm off-white for text: **#F6F2E8**
* Muted sand for secondary text: **#978D79**

Typefaces: **Source Serif 4** for headlines, **Plus Jakarta Sans** for
everything else.

**LOOK AND FEEL**
Warm, premium, confident, calm. Gold light on dark warm charcoal — think gold
leaf, brass, lamplight, late afternoon sun, not neon or glitter. Real people in
real Guwahati homes and streets. Grain and warmth rather than gloss.

Assamese context is welcome when it is genuine: the Brahmaputra, monsoon green,
the hills behind the city, auto-rickshaws, a gamosa (white cotton with a red
woven border), a mekhela sador. Use these because they are true, not as
decoration.

Tone of voice: plain, warm, direct, honest. Short sentences. No hype, no
exclamation marks, no corporate filler. Say the thing and stop. The brand
admits what it is not.

**CASTING**
Northeast Indian and Assamese faces. Everyday clothes people actually wear to
work — not costumes, not branded uniforms. A mix of men and women, ages 20 to
55. Ordinary, capable, warm. Never glossy models pretending to plumb.

**NEVER SHOW OR SAY**
* Never imply the workers are Nearse employees or wear a Nearse uniform. They
  are independent. Nearse is a place to find each other, not an employer.
* Never say "verified", "background checked" or "police verified". Nearse
  checks that a profile looks genuine. It is not a background check, and
  claiming otherwise is a legal problem.
* Never show payment, cards, UPI, wallets or cash passing through Nearse. It
  takes no payments and asks for no payment details. This is a selling point,
  not an omission.
* Never invent ratings, worker counts, download numbers or testimonials.
* Never show workers as pitiable or desperate. Dignity, always.
* Never claim availability outside Guwahati. It is Guwahati only right now.
* Never promise a guaranteed arrival time, a fixed price for a job, or a
  refund. The price is agreed between the two people.
* Do not use the phrase "gig" or "gig worker".

**HONEST FRAMING**
Nearse is on an experimental launch and is new. Confident, not boastful.
"Now live in Guwahati", not "India's number one".

**CONTACT**
Website: nearse.in · WhatsApp: +91 70865 99367 · Guwahati, Assam

## Getting good results

**Leave text out of AI images.** Every image generator mangles Assamese and
Devanagari, and most mangle English past a few words. Generate the picture
clean, then put the headline, the logo and nearse.in on top in Canva. The
typefaces are Source Serif 4 for headlines and Plus Jakarta Sans for the rest.

**Ask for one idea per image.** "An electrician being welcomed at a door in
Beltola" beats "people using an app for many services".

**Name the locality.** "A flat in Ganeshguri", "a lane in Uzan Bazar", "a
morning market in Fancy Bazar" — specificity is what stops an image looking
like it could be anywhere.

**Hands and tools are where AI fails.** Prefer framing where hands are relaxed
or partly out of shot, or generate a few and pick.

**For video**, add the pacing yourself: "30 seconds, 6 shots, calm handheld,
one line of voiceover per shot, Assamese with English subtitles."

**Angles that work for this brand**
* The worker's side — someone good at their trade, finally findable.
* The customer's relief — the fan gets fixed, the tutor turns up.
* The zero-commission fact stated plainly on a gold card.
* A locality name as the hero: "Now in Beltola."
* The three-month free trial for workers, stressing no card is asked for.

**Before publishing anything**, check it against the NEVER list above. The two
that matter most: no Nearse uniforms, and never the word "verified" about a
person.
